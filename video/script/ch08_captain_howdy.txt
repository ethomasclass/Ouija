Rewind to 1949. In Maryland, just outside Washington, D.C., a family says something is wrong with their teenage son. Strange noises. Furniture moving on its own. According to some accounts, it all started after his aunt, a Spiritualist, taught him to use a Ouija board.

The family turns to the Catholic Church, and priests perform an exorcism, much of it in St. Louis. Yes, St. Louis again. A college student at Georgetown named William Peter Blatty reads about it in The Washington Post. He never forgets it.

Twenty-two years later, he turns it into a novel. And in December 1973, it becomes a movie: The Exorcist.

In the movie, 12-year-old Regan finds a Ouija board in the basement and plays with it alone. She tells her mom about her new friend. "I make the questions," she says, "and he does the answers."

People lined up around the block to see it. Some fainted in the theaters. It became one of the biggest movies ever made.

The Ouija historian Robert Murch compares it to the movie Psycho: "No one was afraid of showers until that scene."

Now, The Exorcist didn't invent the fear. Some religious leaders had warned people away from Spiritualism and talking boards for decades. What the movie did was hand that fear to everyone at once. Millions of people. One story. And now, the board had a villain.

After that, Ouija boards turn up in horror movie after horror movie. And in 2001, in Alamogordo, New Mexico, a church held a bonfire and burned Ouija boards, along with Harry Potter books and Disney's Snow White.

But here's the twist. Who was selling the fear? In 2014, a horror movie called Ouija hit theaters. One of the companies that made it was Hasbro, which owns Parker Brothers. It cost somewhere between five and eight million dollars, and it made over a hundred million. The toy company made a horror movie about its own toy.

And just six years earlier, Hasbro had put out a pink Ouija board, aimed at girls ages eight and up. Parents were furious. Same company, same years: the Ouija board as a slumber-party toy, and the Ouija board as a doorway for demons.
