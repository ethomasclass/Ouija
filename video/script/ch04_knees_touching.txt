So who was buying it? A lot of young couples.

Dating around 1900 came with rules. A young man and woman couldn't just be alone together. Parents watched. Neighbors talked.

Now look at how you play Ouija. Two people, facing each other, the board across both your laps. Knees touching. Lights low. Fingertips on the same little pointer. Then you ask questions. Does he like me? Will we get married?

It's a parlor game with a built-in excuse to touch. And if the board says yes... who's going to argue with the spirits?

By 1920, America is in a full Ouija craze. The summer before, Rockwell had watched couples at a dance hall in Potsdam, New York, sitting knee to knee over Ouija boards. That's where his cover came from.
