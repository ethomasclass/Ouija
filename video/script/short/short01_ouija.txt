In 1920, this was a date.

Fifty-three years later, it was a demon.

Same board. Same alphabet. Same YES and NO. So what changed?

It was patented in 1891 as a "Toy or Game." Couples played it knees touching. After World War One and the 1918 flu, mothers used it to reach sons who weren't coming home. In 1967, it outsold Monopoly. Then The Exorcist handed everyone the fear.

But who actually moves the pointer? Scientists call it the ideomotor effect: your muscles move with an idea before you decide to move them. In a 2012 study, players who thought they were guessing did better through the board than out loud.

The board never changed. The story did.
