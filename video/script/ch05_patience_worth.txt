But at the same time, other people were taking it dead seriously.

July 8th, 1913. St. Louis. A housewife named Pearl Curran sits at a Ouija board with a friend, and the pointer spells out:

"Many moons ago I lived. Again I come. Patience Worth my name."

Patience said she was an Englishwoman from the 1600s. Over the next twenty-four years, Pearl produced around four million words in her name. Novels, plays, thousands of poems. Most researchers think Patience came from Pearl's own mind. But to her readers, it looked like proof.

So the same board that's a flirting game in one parlor is, in St. Louis, a doorway to a voice from three hundred years ago.
